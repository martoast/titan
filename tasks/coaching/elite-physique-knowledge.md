# Elite Physique Knowledge — the research behind the coach's playbook

This is the source material behind `app/Support/TrainingPlaybook.php` (the `coaching_playbook` coach
tool). It distils the published methods of the bodybuilding greats and the modern hypertrophy evidence
base into coaching principles, so Titan can coach **advanced, ambitious athletes** — not just the
average person — while staying strictly natural and evidence-based.

> **Hard rail (encoded in the playbook + system prompt):** professional bodybuilders at this level almost
> universally use anabolic steroids, GH, insulin and diuretics. Titan coaches **natural, evidence-based
> methods only** and **never** prescribes, doses, sources, or designs PEDs / SARMs / diuretics / insulin /
> aggressive water-cuts. We translate the legends' *methods, intensity and mindset* into what a natural
> athlete can actually use, and flag which famous practices only worked because the men doing them were
> enhanced.

## Part 1 — The legends (documented philosophies)

| Figure | School | Core tenet | Transfers to naturals | Drug-dependent (don't copy literally) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arnold Schwarzenegger** (*Encyclopedia of Modern Bodybuilding*) | High volume | Volume + intensity + mind-muscle connection; "shock principle" (variation), supersets, the pump, weak-point priority, posing | Mind-muscle connection, weak-point blocks, modest variation, posing/visualisation | 20–30 sets/muscle, daily-double splits |
| **Mike Mentzer** (Heavy Duty / HIT) | Minimalist HIT | Intensity, not volume: ONE all-out set to failure; train infrequently; **you grow in recovery** | Recovery-first logic, genuine hard effort, low-volume minimalism for busy/older lifters | Single-set-to-failure as *optimal* (it's sufficient, rarely optimal) |
| **Dorian Yates** (Blood and Guts) | Refined HIT | Warm-ups then ONE working set to failure *and beyond*; control, machines, ~1×/wk | Form/control, machines for safe failure, time-efficient brutal sets — one of the most natural-applicable pro methods | Olympia-level mass + recovery |
| **Jay Cutler + Hany Rambod** (FST-7) | High volume | **7 sets × 8–15 reps, 30–45 s rest** to finish a muscle (pump/"fascia stretch"); balance & symmetry | FST-7 as a *finisher* on a lagging muscle, on top of heavy work | Doing it on every muscle every session |
| **Mike O'Hearn** (Power Bodybuilding) | Strength-led | Build on heavy compound strength + progressive overload; decades of consistency, joint care | Strongly — strength trend = best overload proxy; longevity/consistency | (claims natural; method is the most natural-credible template) |
| **Ronnie Coleman** | Heavy maximalism | Intensity + high frequency + maximal free weights, "max weight, max form" | The *philosophy* (heavy base, train often, never skip) | 40+ sets/session, ~2 h, 8× Olympia mass |

**Volume spectrum:** Mentzer/Yates (1 set to failure) ←→ O'Hearn (strength-led moderate) ←→ FST-7 / Arnold
/ Coleman (high-volume pump). **The natural sweet spot for advanced trainees sits left-of-centre of where
the pros actually trained.**

## Part 2 — The modern evidence base (the backbone)

**Primary driver — mechanical tension.** Hard, controlled reps through a meaningful ROM taken near failure
are *the* growth signal; pump (metabolic stress) and damage are secondary.

- **Volume** is the main dose knob — weekly hard sets per muscle, up to a recoverable ceiling.
  **RP landmarks:** MV ~6–10 · **MEV ~8–12** · **MAV ~12–20 (where most growth lives)** · **MRV ~20+**
  (chronic ceiling). Start a meso near MEV, add ~1–2 sets/wk toward MRV, then deload.
- **Load / reps:** hypertrophy is similar across ~**5–30 reps** *if taken close to failure*; ~6–12 is the
  efficient meat. Load is a tool, not the stimulus.
- **Proximity to failure (RIR):** growth rises as you approach failure, but **~0–3 RIR captures most of it**;
  the last rep isn't worth its fatigue on most work. **Strength is largely insensitive** to proximity to
  failure. Push *isolation/machine* work to/near failure; keep **0–3 RIR on big compounds** (joints, form,
  systemic fatigue). Yates was right to take failure on machines.
- **Frequency** mostly distributes volume — **2×/muscle/week** is a robust advanced default; 1× (HIT) or 3×
  only to serve volume/recovery.
- **ROM / lengthened position (big modern theme):** train full ROM emphasising the **stretched** position;
  **lengthened partials** match or beat full-ROM hypertrophy and are a legit advanced tool.
- **Tempo:** control the eccentric (~1–3 s); don't grind ultra-slow concentrics (it just cuts load/reps).
- **Progressive overload:** double progression (reps → load); strength trend on key lifts is the cleanest
  objective overload signal for naturals.

**Periodization (advanced):** 4–8 wk hypertrophy mesos (3–6 wk strength), accumulate volume → **deload**
(~1 wk, sets −30–50%) → reset higher. **Deload triggers:** stalled/regressing lifts, achy joints, poor
sleep, elevated resting HR, motivation drop, can't hit prescribed RIR. **Specialisation:** prioritise a
lagging muscle (trained first, fresh, more volume) while others sit at maintenance — can't grow everything
maximally at once.

**Intensity techniques (targeted finishers, 1–3 sets/muscle, mostly isolation, late in a meso):**
drop sets, rest-pause, **myo-reps** (one activation set near failure, then mini-sets of 3–5 with 3–5
breaths — same stimulus, ~70% less time), supersets/giant sets, lengthened partials, forced reps/negatives
(potent, very fatiguing — sparingly), FST-7. They *concentrate fatigue* — seasoning, not the meal.

**Nutrition:**
- **Protein** ~**1.6–2.2 g/kg/day** captures ~all benefit; **deep cut ~2.3–3.1 g/kg** to protect muscle.
  Spread over 3–5 meals; total >> timing.
- **Lean bulk:** small surplus (~+5–15% / +200–300 kcal), **~0.25–0.5% bw gain/wk**; advanced naturals gain
  slowly and must bulk lean. **Cut:** ~−300–500 kcal / ~0.5–1% bw/wk, slower when lean.
- **Refeeds / diet breaks** on long cuts help hormones, training and adherence. **"Lean enough to grow"**
  (~10–15% men off-season) improves partitioning and keeps preps short.
- **Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day** is the one near-universal evidence-backed aid; the rest is mostly noise.
- **Contest prep is phased:** off-season (build) → prep (sustained moderate deficit, protein high, intensity
  held, volume drops) → peak week → reverse-diet out.

**Recovery:** sleep (7–9 h) is the master variable; manage systemic/CNS fatigue (MRV is a fatigue ceiling);
deload on the triggers above. *Adaptation happens in recovery, not the workout* — the one point HIT and
modern science fully agree on.

**Peak week (high-level, heavy caution):** final ~5–7 days manipulate carbs (deplete → load), water and
sodium to maximise fullness/definition for one day. **Risky, individual, easy to ruin** — originated on the
drug side. The honest version: the diet does 95% weeks out; peak week only fine-tunes. **Titan will explain
the concepts and get you lean + full via training/diet, but will not prescribe water/electrolyte/diuretic
protocols** — that needs an experienced in-person coach and medical oversight.

## Part 3 — Natural vs enhanced (the line Titan holds)

- **Transfers to naturals:** mechanical tension + 0–3 RIR, MEV→MRV volume at *natural* levels (~10–20
  sets/muscle/wk, **not** 40+), 2× frequency, lengthened-position ROM, intensity techniques as finishers,
  double progression, mesos + deloads, weak-point blocks, lean-bulk / controlled-cut nutrition, protein
  1.6–2.2 (higher cutting), creatine, sleep-first recovery, consistency.
- **Drug-enabled (do NOT replicate):** 20–40+ set sessions and daily doubles (Arnold/Coleman), near-daily
  failure on heavy compounds, Olympia mass/recovery rates, aggressive peak-week water/diuretic protocols.
- **Set natural expectations:** muscle gain is measured in months/years; bulk lean; consistency and recovery
  beat chasing pro volumes. If someone's on a doctor-supervised protocol (e.g. TRT), coach the training and
  nutrition around it and send medication questions to a physician.

## Glute growth & waistline definition (women's most-requested goal)

**Glutes — train both heads.** *Gluteus maximus* = the mass/projection (hip EXTENSION): hip thrust (highest
mean activation, the lift to progressively overload), hinges (RDL, 45° back extension, pull-through), squats,
lunges. *Gluteus medius/minimus* = the upper-outer **"shelf"/3D roundness** (hip ABDUCTION): machine/cable/
banded abduction, frog pumps, single-leg work — neglecting it is the #1 reason a glute is strong but flat on top.

**The spectrum, every glute week** (Contreras' activator/stretcher/pumper):
- **Heavy activators** — hip thrust ~4×6 (pause + squeeze, ribs down), deadlift. Recover ~2–3 d.
- **Stretchers** — Bulgarian split squat, deficit lunge, deep/sumo squat, RDL ~4×8 slow eccentric (load the
  lengthened position). Most growth per rep, most soreness; recover 3–4 d.
- **Pumpers** — abduction, kickback, banded thrust, frog pump ~3×15–30, short rest. Low soreness, recover
  1–2 d → can run near-daily in a specialization block.

**Volume/frequency:** glutes recover fast — ~12–20+ hard sets/week, 2–4×/week (pump sessions can push higher).
Spread volume across days; heavy/stretch when fresh, pump when fatigued. **Mistakes:** quad-dominant squatting,
partial ROM, only heavy, skipping abduction, lower-back/anterior-tilt stealing the work.

**Waistline — fat, control, and ratio (not endless ab work):**
- Spot reduction is a **myth** — a defined waist comes from getting LEAN (energy balance). Core work shapes,
  it doesn't strip waist fat.
- For a tight/flat look train **control**: anti-rotation (Pallof press), anti-extension (plank, dead bug),
  stomach **vacuums** (TVA). Keep oblique work **light/unloaded** — heavy loaded side bends / weighted twists
  can thicken the obliques and **widen** the waist.
- Biggest visual win is **ratio**: building the **glutes/hips** (waist-to-hip ~0.7) and **shoulders + lats**
  makes the waist read smaller by contrast. Posture tightens it instantly.

pump spectrum, abduction forced as the focus finisher, waist-friendly core, region-aware placement).
